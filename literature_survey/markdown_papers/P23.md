## Abstract
With the growing demand for computational services, data centers (DCs) have become major electricity consumers, contributing significantly to carbon emissions and posing new challenges to power system flexibility and sustainability. This study addresses the intraday scheduling of DCs under high-frequency fluctuations caused by renewable integration and load uncertainty. A carbon-aware bi-level optimization framework is proposed: the upper level clears the market based on electricity prices and carbon intensity, while the lower level minimizes the total operating cost, including electricity procurement, carbon emissions, and task migration costs. To overcome the real-time challenges of intraday decision-making, the problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), and a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is developed. The agent interacts with the environment to dynamically adjust power procurement and task scheduling. The convergence and training stability of the proposed method are validated through representative learning metrics, including mean reward, explained variance, total loss, and value loss. Simulation results on a modified IEEE 5-bus system show that the approach outperforms traditional day-ahead strategies, reducing costs and emissions while enhancing spatiotemporal coordination. The PPO algorithm also exhibits a better performance than other benchmark DRL methods. Overall, the DRL-based carbon-aware scheduling strategy offers promising potential for advancing low-carbon and resilient power systems.

## 1. Introduction & Motivation

The exponential growth of hyperscale cloud services, big data analytics, and artificial intelligence model training has accelerated the power consumption of modern data centers. Globally, data centers represent a growing share of electricity consumption and associated greenhouse gas emissions.

### 1.1 The Intraday Grid Context
1. **High-Frequency Fluctuations:** Modern electric power systems increasingly incorporate high shares of intermittent renewable energy resources (such as wind and solar generation). Consequently, wholesale electricity prices (locational marginal prices, LMP) and grid carbon emission intensities vary dynamically at sub-hourly and hourly timescales.
2. **Shortcomings of Day-Ahead Scheduling:** Traditional data center energy management relies on deterministic day-ahead scheduling, which plans energy procurement 24 hours in advance. Day-ahead plans cannot adapt to intraday renewable forecast errors, real-time grid congestion, or sudden price/carbon spikes.
3. **Coupled Power and Computing Flexibility:** Data centers possess unique operational flexibility: computational workloads can be shifted temporally (delaying batch processing) and migrated spatially (rerouting jobs across geographically distributed data centers connected to different grid buses).

### 1.2 Core Contributions
- **Bi-Level Carbon-Aware Optimization:** Formulates a hierarchical market framework where the upper level handles wholesale market clearing under carbon intensity signals, and the lower level optimizes data center operational costs, emissions, and computational task migration.
- **PPO-Based Real-Time Decision-Making:** Overcomes the computational intractability of solving real-time bi-level mathematical programs by training a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent that outputs power procurement bids and task migration schedules in real time.
- **Spatiotemporal Load Shifting:** Coordinated spatial migration across electric power buses to exploit regional differences in renewable availability and locational carbon intensity.

## 2. Mathematical System Model & Problem Formulation

### 2.1 Bi-Level Architecture
- **Upper Level (Electricity Market Clearing):**
  - Manages market clearing based on Locational Marginal Prices (LMPs) and nodal carbon intensity signals ($CI_i(t)$ in $g\text{CO}_2/\text{kWh}$).
  - Enforces power flow constraints, generator capacity limits, and transmission line ratings across the power grid.
- **Lower Level (Data Center Operating Cost Minimization):**
  - Objective:
    $$\min \sum_{t=1}^{T} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{DC}} \left[ C_{\text{elec}, i}(t) + C_{\text{carbon}, i}(t) + C_{\text{mig}, i}(t) \right]$$
  - Where:
    - $C_{\text{elec}, i}(t) = p_i(t) \cdot P_{\text{dc}, i}(t) \cdot \Delta t$ represents wholesale electricity procurement costs.
    - $C_{\text{carbon}, i}(t) = \alpha_{\text{carbon}} \cdot CI_i(t) \cdot P_{\text{dc}, i}(t) \cdot \Delta t$ represents carbon emission costs/penalties under carbon tax or cap-and-trade mechanisms.
    - $C_{\text{mig}, i}(t) = \sum_{j \ne i} \beta_{\text{mig}} \cdot W_{i \to j}(t)$ accounts for WAN bandwidth expenses and service latency penalties incurred during inter-DC task migration.

### 2.2 Constraints
- **Data Center Power Balance:** $P_{\text{dc}, i}(t) = P_{\text{IT}, i}(t) + P_{\text{cool}, i}(t)$, with Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) modeling.
- **Workload Conservation:** $\sum_{t} W_i(t) + \sum_{j \ne i} W_{j \to i}(t) - \sum_{j \ne i} W_{i \to j}(t) \ge D_{\text{total}, i}$.
- **Delay Tolerance & Deadlines:** Tasks must be completed within permissible deadline slack windows.

## 3. Deep Reinforcement Learning (PPO) Framework

### 3.1 Markov Decision Process (MDP)
- **State Space ($S_t$):**
  - Grid state: Nodal LMPs, locational carbon intensities, renewable generation outputs, and transmission line loadings across all buses.
  - Data center state: Pending computational task queue depths, current IT power consumption, server utilization levels, and available computing capacity.
  - Temporal features: Time-of-day and dispatch interval indices.
- **Action Space ($A_t$):**
  - Continuous power procurement bids for intraday market submission.
  - Spatial task migration volume matrices: $W_{i \to j}(t)$ routing workload from data center $i$ to data center $j$.
- **Reward Function ($R_t$):**
  - Formulated as the negative total operating expenditure:
    $$R_t = - \left( C_{\text{elec}}(t) + \omega_c C_{\text{carbon}}(t) + \omega_m C_{\text{mig}}(t) + \text{Penalty}_{\text{SLA}}(t) \right)$$
  - Directly incentivizes the agent to maximize renewable utilization, minimize carbon footprints, and avoid SLA violations.

### 3.2 Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) Setup
- **Clipped Surrogate Objective:**
  $$L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t) \right]$$
- **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE):** Balances bias and variance in policy gradients.
- **Actor-Critic Architecture:** Shared multi-layer perceptron feature extractor with separate actor (policy) and critic (value function) heads.

## 4. Simulation Evaluation & Numerical Results

### 4.1 Testbed Setup
- **Power System:** Modified IEEE 5-bus transmission system incorporating wind generation, solar PV farms, conventional thermal units, and multiple geo-distributed data centers.
- **Workload Traces:** Realistic data center workload arrival patterns capturing diurnal batch analytics and real-time interactive computational requests.
- **Market Dynamics:** Intraday rolling market clearing intervals simulating real-time grid conditions.

### 4.2 Baselines Compared
1. **Day-Ahead Deterministic Scheduling:** Conventional baseline optimizing schedules 24 hours in advance without intraday adaptation.
2. **Benchmark DRL Algorithms:** Standard DRL methods including DQN and continuous-action actor-critic baselines.
3. **PPO (Proposed):** Carbon-aware bi-level intraday scheduling.

### 4.3 Key Results & Convergence
- **Cost and Emission Reductions:** The PPO-based strategy significantly reduces total operating costs and carbon emissions compared to deterministic day-ahead strategies.
- **Spatiotemporal Synergy:** Effectively migrates computational loads to buses with abundant local renewable generation and low carbon intensity, relieving grid transmission congestion.
- **Training Stability:** PPO demonstrates superior convergence stability, validated through high explained variance, low value loss, and steady mean episodic reward growth.

## 5. Limitations
1. **Test System Scale:** Evaluated on a modified IEEE 5-bus system; scaling to large-scale interconnection grids (e.g., IEEE 118-bus or real ISO footprints) requires hierarchical or multi-agent extensions.
2. **Simplified IT Power Model:** Uses aggregate IT power curves rather than detailed chip-level DVFS and heterogeneous GPU/CPU microarchitectural models.
3. **Closed Source:** As typical of IEEE Transactions journal publications, source code and environment configurations are not publicly hosted on GitHub.
