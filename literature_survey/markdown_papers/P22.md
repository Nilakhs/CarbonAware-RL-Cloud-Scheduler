## Abstract
High-Performance Computing (HPC) clusters consume massive amounts of electrical power and produce significant carbon footprints. While co-locating HPC clusters with intermittent on-site renewable energy sources (such as wind turbines and solar photovoltaic panels) offers a promising decarbonization pathway, the inherent volatility of renewable generation poses severe scheduling challenges. Traditional HPC job schedulers prioritize user-facing performance metrics (e.g., job wait time, turnaround time, bounded slowdown) through priority queues and heuristic backfilling, ignoring dynamic green power availability. Existing single-action Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) schedulers only make job selection decisions, dispatching jobs immediately upon selection and causing severe mismatch with fluctuating renewable power profiles.

To resolve this conflict, we propose **GAS-MARL** (Green-Aware job Scheduling algorithm based on Multi-Action Deep Reinforcement Learning), a coordinated multi-action DRL scheduling framework for green HPC clusters. GAS-MARL formulates job scheduling as a multi-action Markov Decision Process where the agent simultaneously executes two coordinated decisions in each decision epoch:
1. **Action 1 (Job Selection):** Selects which job from the waiting queue to schedule using resource feasibility masking.
2. **Action 2 (Delay / Timing Decision):** Determines whether to dispatch the job immediately, delay execution until currently running jobs complete (resource-release delay), or delay execution for a discrete duration to wait for an upcoming green power surplus window (green-power delay).

Furthermore, we develop **Green-Backfilling**, an innovative backfilling policy that allows smaller, lower-power jobs from the queue to backfill idle nodes during delay windows without delaying the start time of the delayed primary job. Extensive trace-driven evaluations using standard HPC production logs (SDSC SP2, LLNL T3D) and empirical renewable traces demonstrate that GAS-MARL substantially increases renewable energy utilization while maintaining competitive average bounded slowdown compared to heuristic schedulers (FCFS, F2/WFP3, LPTPN), genetic algorithms, and single-action Maskable PPO baselines.

## 1. Introduction & Motivation

High-Performance Computing (HPC) facilities are indispensable engines for scientific discovery, climate modeling, AI research, and engineering simulations. However, their computational capacity requires megawatts of electrical power, resulting in substantial operating costs and carbon footprints.

### 1.1 Challenges in Green HPC Scheduling
1. **Renewable Energy Intermittency:** Integrating on-site solar photovoltaic (PV) arrays and wind turbines creates highly volatile power generation curves that fluctuate with weather conditions and diurnal cycles.
2. **Performance vs. Green Trade-offs:** Stalling jobs to wait for peak solar or wind production can severely increase job queue latency and average bounded slowdown, breaching user expectations and service level objectives.
3. **Limitations of Single-Action DRL Schedulers:** Prior DRL-based cloud/HPC schedulers (e.g., DeepJS, Decima, RLScheduler) operate on a single-action paradigm: the policy network selects a job from the queue and immediately dispatches it to available cluster nodes. If the system is in a renewable power trough, the newly dispatched job consumes expensive and carbon-intensive grid (brown) electricity.

### 1.2 Core Contributions of GAS-MARL
- **Multi-Action Decision Coordination:** Decouples job selection from execution timing. The agent selects a candidate job and then determines the optimal timing modality (immediate execution, resource delay, or renewable surplus delay).
- **Green-Backfilling Strategy:** Extends classic EASY-Backfilling to support green power reservations, preventing cluster idle power waste while preserving delayed job start reservations.
- **Multi-Encoder Policy Architecture:** Integrates parallel neural encoders for waiting queue status, running job allocations, and lookahead renewable generation forecasts.
- **Open-Source Artifacts:** All source code, simulation environments, neural network definitions, and evaluation datasets are publicly released at `https://github.com/sioncr/green-rl-sched`.

## 2. System Architecture & Problem Formulation

### 2.1 HPC Cluster Model
- **Cluster Hardware:** A homogeneous or partitioned HPC cluster consisting of $N$ compute nodes, each containing $M$ processor cores (e.g., 8 processors per machine).
- **Power Model:**
  - Machine idle power: $P_{\text{idle}} = 50\text{ W}$ per machine.
  - Baseline static cluster power: $P_{\text{stat}} = P_{\text{idle}} \times \lceil N / M \rceil$.
  - Dynamic job power: $P_{\text{job}} \in [16, 50]\text{ W}$ per node, dynamically tracked across discrete power slots.
- **On-Site Renewable Power System:**
  - Wind turbine generator: Rated nominal power $P_{\text{nominal}} = 7,200\text{ W}$, cut-in wind speed $v_{\text{in}} = 2.5\text{ m/s}$, rated speed $v_{\text{rated}} = 15\text{ m/s}$, cut-off speed $v_{\text{off}} = 30\text{ m/s}$.
  - Solar photovoltaic array: $N_{\text{PV}} = 200$ panels with efficiency $\eta_{\text{PV}} = 0.20$.
  - Hourly meteorological traces: Replayed from real-world wind speed and solar irradiance datasets (`windspeedS.csv`, `irradianceS.csv`).
  - Total renewable power at time $t$: $P_{\text{green}}(t) = P_{\text{solar}}(t) + P_{\text{wind}}(t)$.

### 2.2 Workload Model
- Jobs are formatted according to the Standard Workload Format (SWF) with attributes:
  - Submit time $r_i$
  - Requested processor count $m_i$
  - Requested run time $\tau_i^{\text{req}}$
  - Actual run time $\tau_i$
  - Allocated machine set and start time $s_i$
- **Evaluation Traces:**
  - `lublin_256.swf`: Synthetic 256-node workload generated via Lublin99 model.
  - `Cirne.swf`: Production log from San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC SP2, 128 nodes).
  - `Jann.swf`: Production log from Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL T3D, 256 nodes).

## 3. The GAS-MARL Framework

### 3.1 State Representation
The composite observation vector incorporates three distinct information channels:
$$\mathbf{s}_t = [\mathbf{s}_{\text{queue}}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{running}}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{green}}]$$
- **Waiting Queue State ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{queue}}$):** Up to $MAX\_QUEUE\_SIZE = 256$ jobs, each described by 8 normalized features:
  1. Normalized waiting time: $\min\left(\frac{t - r_i}{MAX\_WAIT\_TIME}, 1.0\right)$
  2. Normalized requested execution time: $\min\left(\frac{\tau_i^{\text{req}}}{\max\_exec\_time}, 1.0\right)$
  3. Normalized processor demand: $\min\left(\frac{m_i}{\max\_procs}, 1.0\right)$
  4. Normalized total job power: $\min\left(\frac{P_i}{MAX\_POWER}, 1.0\right)$
  5. Normalized per-processor power: $\min\left(\frac{P_i / m_i}{MAX\_perProcPower}, 1.0\right)$
  6. Estimated brown energy consumption increment $\Delta E_{\text{brown}}$ if scheduled immediately
  7. Estimated green energy utilization increment $\Delta E_{\text{green}}$ if scheduled immediately
  8. Allocatability indicator: $1.0$ if available free nodes $\ge m_i$, else $0.0$.
- **Running Jobs State ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{running}}$):** Up to $run\_win = 64$ currently running jobs, tracking allocated processor count, power, per-processor power, and remaining execution time.
- **Green Power Profile ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{green}}$):** $green\_win = 24$ hourly lookahead slots, tracking slot remaining duration and normalized predicted renewable power.

### 3.2 Action Space
Each scheduling step consists of a coordinated action pair $(a_1, a_2)$:
- **Action 1 (Job Selection):** $a_1 \in \{0, \dots, MAX\_QUEUE\_SIZE - 1\}$. Selects the job $j^*$ from the waiting queue. A boolean mask invalidates non-existent jobs or jobs that cannot fit cluster capacity.
- **Action 2 (Delay / Execution Timing Decision):**
  - $a_2 = 0$: **Immediate execution** without delay.
  - $a_2 \in \{1, \dots, delayMaxJobNum\}$ (where $delayMaxJobNum = 5$): **Resource-release delay**. Fast-forwards simulation time to the release of nodes from the $a_2$-th running job.
  - $a_2 \in \{delayMaxJobNum + 1, \dots, |DS| + delayMaxJobNum\}$: **Green-power delay**. Delays job start by a discrete time chosen from candidate list $DS = [300, 600, 1200, 1800, 2400, 3000, 3600]$ seconds to align with upcoming green power availability.

### 3.3 Green-Backfilling
During delay intervals, the cluster may contain idle nodes. To avoid resource wastage:
1. The scheduler establishes a reservation for the delayed primary job at its scheduled start time $t_{\text{target}}$.
2. Backfill candidate jobs from the waiting queue are evaluated in order.
3. A candidate job is allowed to run immediately if:
   - It requires $\le$ current free nodes, AND
   - Its requested execution time does not collide with the primary job reservation ($t + \tau_{\text{backfill}} \le t_{\text{target}}$).

### 3.4 Neural Network Architecture
- **JobEncoder:** MLP [input_dim $\rightarrow$ 64 $\rightarrow$ ReLU $\rightarrow$ 128 $\rightarrow$ ReLU].
- **RunningJobEncoder:** MLP [input_dim $\rightarrow$ 64 $\rightarrow$ ReLU $\rightarrow$ 128 $\rightarrow$ ReLU].
- **GreenEncoder:** MLP [input_dim $\rightarrow$ 64 $\rightarrow$ ReLU $\rightarrow$ 128 $\rightarrow$ ReLU].
- **Decoder 1 (Action 1 Policy Head):** Linear [128 $\rightarrow$ 64 $\rightarrow$ 16 $\rightarrow$ 1], outputs logits over queue positions masked by invalid action mask $M_1$.
- **JobEmbedding:** Linear [8 $\rightarrow$ 128], embeds the job selected by Action 1.
- **Decoder 2 (Action 2 Policy Head):** Concatenates all encoder states with the selected job embedding, projects via MLP [64 $\rightarrow$ action2_num], masked by invalid delay mask $M_2$.
- **CriticNet:** Value function network estimating state value $V(s)$ from concatenated multi-encoder embeddings.

### 3.5 Objective & Reward Function
The learning objective maximizes a weighted sum of execution performance and renewable energy utilization:
$$R = \eta \cdot r_{\text{perf}} + U_{\text{green}}$$
where:
- Performance penalty: $r_{\text{perf}} = - \text{bsld}(j) = - \max\left(1.0, \frac{C_j - r_j}{\max(\tau_j, 10)}\right)$ (negative bounded slowdown).
- Renewable energy utilization:
  $$U_{\text{green}} = \frac{E_{\text{green}}}{E_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\sum_{t} \min(P_{\text{cluster}}(t), P_{\text{green}}(t)) \Delta t}{\sum_{t} P_{\text{cluster}}(t) \Delta t}$$
- $\eta = 0.002$: Balances the magnitude of bounded slowdown against fractional renewable utilization ($[0, 1]$).

## 4. Experimental Evaluation

### 4.1 Baselines Evaluated
1. **FCFS (First-Come First-Served):** Non-preemptive FIFO scheduling.
2. **F2 (WFP3 / SJF heuristic):** Heuristic favoring shorter jobs to minimize wait times.
3. **LPTPN (Longest Processing Time per Processor Node):** Heuristic prioritizing resource-dense jobs.
4. **GA (Genetic Algorithm):** Metaheuristic optimizing job sequence via a two-queue initialization strategy.
5. **MaskablePPO (Single-Action DRL):** Standard PPO scheduler with action masking that only selects jobs without delay capability.
6. **GAS-MARL (Proposed):** Multi-action DRL coordinating job selection and delay with Green-Backfilling.

### 4.2 Backfilling Ablations
- Policy 0: No backfilling.
- Policy 1: Green-Backfilling (proposed).
- Policy 2: EASY-Backfilling (standard production baseline).

### 4.3 Key Findings
- **Renewable Utilization Gain:** GAS-MARL significantly outperforms heuristic and single-action DRL baselines in renewable energy utilization by matching job start times with renewable generation peaks.
- **Bounded Slowdown Control:** By restricting delay options to discrete short-term windows ($DS \le 3600\text{ s}$) and conditioning delay on the specific job power profile, GAS-MARL avoids the severe queue stall penalties of naive threshold deferral.
- **Synergy of Multi-Action & Green-Backfilling:** Green-Backfilling recovers compute cycles that would otherwise idle during green delay windows, maintaining high cluster throughput while achieving high green energy matching.

## 5. Limitations & Future Directions
1. **Absence of Regional Grid Carbon Intensity:** The framework models on-site renewable self-consumption ($U_{\text{green}}$) rather than optimizing against dynamic grid marginal emissions rates ($g\text{CO}_2/\text{kWh}$).
2. **Single Cluster Scope:** Focuses on single-site HPC batch clusters; geographic migration across multi-cluster federations is not supported.
3. **Synthetic/Profiled Power:** Job power ratings are sampled from offline profiles (`power.csv`) rather than measured via dynamic online hardware telemetry (e.g., RAPL/IPMI).
